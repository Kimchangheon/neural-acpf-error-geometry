\begin{abstract}
Recent neural power-flow solvers, including emerging foundation models,
achieve accurate voltage predictions, yet such accuracy does not
necessarily imply physically consistent solutions.
Even small voltage errors can yield large AC power-balance residuals.
We study this accuracy--consistency gap across PIGNN-GC, GridSFM,
gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA on realistic 2224-bus GBnetwork scenarios,
with cross-grid evaluation of GridSFM over 31 systems.
Using an SVD basis fitted to training AC power-flow solutions, we find
that neural prediction errors contain substantial components outside the
dominant solution subspace. To address this mismatch, calibrated
solution-subspace projection (CSP) suppresses off-subspace prediction
components after train-only bias calibration, reducing Mean PB by $67.0\%$, $37.8\%$, $40.5\%$, and $68.9\%$
for PIGNN-GC, GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA, respectively,
relative to calibrated predictions, while improving voltage-magnitude
accuracy in all four models.
These results identify output-error geometry as an important factor in
physics-consistent neural AC power flow.
\end{abstract}







\begin{keywords}
AC power flow, physics-informed neural network, error geometry, calibration, subspace projection
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

AC power flow (AC-PF) is a fundamental tool for steady-state power-system
analysis \cite{zimmerman2011matpower}, repeatedly evaluated in time-series
and contingency studies and underlying optimal power flow (OPF)
\cite{frank2012opf} and state-estimation workflows
\cite{cheng2024stateestimation}. This computational burden has motivated
neural surrogates spanning direct data-driven approximation
\cite{lin2024powerflownet}, physics- or constraint-aware training
\cite{piloto2024canos,wen2026pigat}, learned projection-based correction
\cite{chzhen2026fab}, and feasibility-seeking mechanisms
\cite{nguyen2025fsnet,donti2021dc3}. More recent cross-grid and
foundation-model efforts---including GridSFM \cite{yang2026gridsfm},
gridfm-graphkit and GENCO \cite{gridfmgraphkit2026,puech2026genco},
and LUMINA \cite{li2026lumina}---extend this direction with global context
and rich, heterogeneous, topology-aware representations designed to operate
across diverse grids and operating conditions. However, accurate voltage
prediction does not necessarily imply AC-consistent output
\cite{ma2026safepowergraph}. Physics-informed objectives can reduce
power-flow residuals \cite{nellikkath2022pinn}, but remain soft training
objectives and do not by themselves enforce exact satisfaction of the nonlinear AC equations.
Consequently, small voltage errors can coexist with non-negligible
power-balance violations. This raises a question beyond state accuracy:
which properties of the remaining voltage error govern
AC power-balance residuals?

    \label{fig:graphical_abstract}

We hypothesize that the key factor is the \emph{direction} of the remaining
voltage error: similarly sized errors can induce very different
power-balance residuals depending on their orientation across buses.

Goodwin et al.~\cite{goodwin2026geometry} characterized the differential
geometry and linear approximations of the AC-PF solution manifold. We instead study neural-surrogate error geometry empirically, asking
whether error orientation relative to a training-derived subspace
explains the remaining physical inconsistency. This also differs from
Park et al.~\cite{park2024compact}, who use a PCA-based subspace to
compress and reconstruct AC-OPF outputs during learning; here, the
subspace is used to analyze and post-process frozen AC-PF predictions.

We evaluate PIGNN-GC, GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA on realistic
2224-bus GBnetwork scenarios. From training solutions only, we construct
separate SVD subspaces for voltage magnitude and angle and decompose
calibrated prediction errors into in- and off-subspace components.
Fixed-norm interventions and local Jacobian analysis show that
off-subspace errors are systematically more residual-sensitive.
Motivated by this observation, we introduce \emph{calibrated
solution-subspace projection} (CSP), which combines train-only bias
correction with projection onto the dominant training-supported solution
directions.

Across all four models, CSP reduces AC power-balance residuals while
improving voltage-magnitude accuracy, with model-dependent effects on
angle accuracy. CSP adds negligible computational burden at inference and remains
beneficial when used to initialize one Newton refinement step.
Additional GridSFM evaluation across 31 systems shows that the benefit
extends beyond GBnetwork, whereas distribution-shift experiments reveal
that the training-derived subspace can become mismatched to a shifted
operating distribution.

\section{Error Geometry and CSP}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{AC power-balance consistency and calibration}

For an $N$-bus grid, let $x=[|V|,\theta]\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}$ denote the
voltage state, with $\underline V=|V|\odot e^{\jmath\theta}$.
Given operating condition $u$, a surrogate predicts
$\hat{x}=f_\phi(u)$, from which active and reactive powers $(P,Q)$ are
recomputed using the network admittance model. For setpoints
$(P^{\mathrm{set}},Q^{\mathrm{set}})$, we define
\[
r(\hat x;u)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\Delta P\\ \Delta Q
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
P^{\mathrm{set}}-P\\
Q^{\mathrm{set}}-Q
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathrm{PB}=\sqrt{\Delta P^2+\Delta Q^2}.
\]
% PB is evaluated bus-wise under the standard slack/PV/PQ conventions;
PB uses $\Delta P$ at PV/PQ buses and $\Delta Q$ at PQ buses,
with other components set to zero;
Mean PB and Max PB aggregate over buses and scenarios.

Before geometric analysis, we remove train-only prediction bias:
\[
\hat b=\mathbb{E}_{s\in\mathrm{tr}}[\hat x_s-x_s^\star],
\qquad
\hat x^C=\hat x-\hat b,
\]
with $\hat b$ frozen at test time. Angle offsets use circular differences
after gauge alignment.

\subsection{Error geometry in a training-derived solution subspace}

We construct separate rank-$k$ bases from training Newton--Raphson
solutions. For centered voltage magnitudes $X_V$ and gauge-aligned
angles $X_\theta$,
\begin{equation}
X_V=W_V\Sigma_VU_V^\top,\qquad
X_\theta=W_\theta\Sigma_\theta U_\theta^\top,\qquad
P_k=W_kW_k^\top,
\end{equation}
where $W_k=\operatorname{diag}(W_{V,k},W_{\theta,k})$ contains the
leading $k$ directions of each channel; $k$ is capped by the available
components on smaller systems.
These subspaces capture dominant training-solution variation and define our error coordinates.
For the calibrated error $e=\hat x^C-x^\star$, we decompose
\begin{equation}
e^{\parallel}=P_k e,\qquad
e^{\perp}=(I-P_k)e,
\label{eq:error_decomposition}
\end{equation}
into components within and outside the dominant training-solution
directions. Around a converged reference state,
\begin{equation}
r(x^\star+e;u)
=
J(x^\star;u)e+\mathcal{O}(\|e\|_2^2),
\label{eq:local_residual}
\end{equation}
since $r(x^\star;u)=0$, where $J$ is the AC residual Jacobian.
Thus, equal-norm errors can produce different residuals depending on
their orientation.

\subsection{Calibrated solution-subspace projection}

Motivated by this directional sensitivity, CSP first removes train-estimated
bias and then projects onto the dominant training-solution directions:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\hat x^{\mathrm{CSP}}
=
\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}}
+
P_k\left(\hat x-\hat b-\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}}\right)
}
\label{eq:csp}
\end{equation}
where $\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}}$ is the training-solution mean;
both $\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $P_k$ remain fixed at test time.

Projection can remove prediction error but also discard valid reference
variation. Let
$e^C=\hat x^C-x^\star$ and
$e^{\mathrm{CSP}}=\hat x^{\mathrm{CSP}}-x^\star$.
Since $P_k$ is orthogonal,
\begin{equation}
\|e^{\mathrm{CSP}}\|_2^2-\|e^C\|_2^2
=
\|(I-P_k)(x^\star-\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}})\|_2^2
-\|(I-P_k)e^C\|_2^2 .
\label{eq:tradeoff}
\end{equation}
Hence, CSP improves state accuracy when the removed off-subspace
prediction-error energy exceeds the discarded reference variation.

CSP is a lightweight, data-driven post-hoc output correction, not a projection onto the
nonlinear AC-feasible set, and therefore does not guarantee exact feasibility.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Our primary study uses 37,022 converged pandapower Newton--Raphson
operating points on the 2224-bus GBnetwork, split 1:1:1
for training/validation/test with varying load, generation, and PV setpoints~\cite{rivera2025pfdelta}.
We evaluate PIGNN-GC, our global-context extension of
PIGNN-Attn-LS~\cite{kim2026pignn}, GridSFM~\cite{yang2026gridsfm},
gridfm-graphkit~\cite{gridfmgraphkit2026}, and
LUMINA~\cite{li2026lumina}; GridSFM is also evaluated
cross-grid on 31 systems.


All models are trained from scratch using model-specific stable
initialization, and CSP is evaluated within each frozen model.
% Unless otherwise stated, results are averaged over three model seeds; single-seed diagnostic analyses use one fixed seed.
gridfm-graphkit uses its released architecture/configuration without a
pretrained checkpoint. GridSFM retains the released backbone but is adapted
from AC-OPF to AC-PF by fixing generator dispatch and training only for
voltage-state prediction with PF-specific residuals. LUMINA likewise retains
its released backbone but uses an enhanced AC-PF configuration with
known-variable masking, electrical bus features, and stabilized initialization.
For all models, electrical inputs, admittance, and evaluation use the same
pandapower internal PPC representation
\cite{thurner2018pandapower,zimmerman2011matpower}. All training and neural inference use a single NVIDIA H100 96-GB GPU;
inference time is measured with the largest model-specific batch size
fitting GPU memory.



All calibration statistics and solution bases are fitted on training data
only. From $k\in\{4,8,16,32,64\}$, validation Mean PB selects
$k^\star=16$ without degrading calibrated voltage-magnitude RMSE; this rank is then
frozen for all test and distribution-shift experiments.

Voltage RMSE alone can be small even when a model largely predicts a
static per-bus profile rather than following scenario-dependent voltage
variation~\cite{yang2026gridsfm}. To isolate this scenario-dependent
response, we center each bus as
$\tilde{V}_{is}=V_{is}-\bar{V}_i$ and
$\tilde{\hat{V}}_{is}=\hat{V}_{is}-\bar{\hat{V}}_i$, and report
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{w}}^2
=
\operatorname{corr}^2
\left(\tilde{V}_{is},\tilde{\hat{V}}_{is}\right),
\qquad
a_{\mathrm{w}}
=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}
\left(\tilde{V}_{is},\tilde{\hat{V}}_{is}\right)}
{\operatorname{Var}\left(\tilde{V}_{is}\right)} .
\end{equation}
$R_{\mathrm{w}}^2$ is the squared correlation between the centered
prediction and reference, while $a_{\mathrm{w}}$ measures the magnitude
of the predicted variation relative to the true variation.

\subsection{Fixed-norm directional intervention.}
We vary the orientation of the voltage-magnitude error relative to the
training-derived solution subspace while holding its $\ell_2$ norm fixed,
thereby isolating direction from error magnitude. Starting from the
calibrated prediction, we define
\begin{equation}
\tilde e(\alpha)
=
\|e_V\|_2
\frac{e_V^\parallel+\alpha e_V^\perp}
{\|e_V^\parallel+\alpha e_V^\perp\|_2}.
\label{eq:controlled_geometry}
\end{equation}
Decreasing $\alpha$ suppresses off-subspace error; the control analogously
suppresses the in-subspace component by exchanging
$e_V^\parallel$ and $e_V^\perp$. Phase angles are held fixed and Mean PB
is normalized at $\alpha=1$. Across all four surrogates, suppressing
off-subspace error consistently reduces Relative Mean PB, whereas
suppressing in-subspace error produces the opposite trend
(Fig.~\ref{fig:error_geometry}). Although the effect size varies across models, its direction is consistent.
Because the voltage-magnitude error norm is fixed throughout the intervention,
these results show that error orientation affects AC power-balance violation
independently of error magnitude.

Consistent with the fixed-norm experiment, a complementary local Jacobian
analysis shows that off-subspace perturbations produce larger AC-residual
changes per unit perturbation norm than in-subspace perturbations.
In a model-independent audit of 4,096 paired random directions at 128
held-out operating points, the median paired off-/in-subspace Jacobian
gain ratio is $1{,}563\times$. The same directional sensitivity is
present in the actual calibrated prediction errors: the ratios of median
off- to in-subspace gains are $22.81\times$, $575.83\times$,
$4.69\times$, and $6.51\times$ for PIGNN-GC, GridSFM,
gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA, respectively. The observed error regime is consistent with the first-order expansion in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_residual}, with median linearized-to-nonlinear
residual-change ratios between $0.996$ and $1.009$ across all four models.

\subsection{Specificity of the solution subspace.}
To test whether the observed benefit follows from generic dimensionality
reduction or graph smoothing, we replace the solution-SVD projection in
CSP with matched random, graph-Laplacian, and spatial low-pass projections.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:basis_control}, only the training-solution SVD
improves both voltage-magnitude accuracy and power balance while increasing
$R_{\mathrm{w}}^2$ from $.685$ to $.853$ and nearly preserving
$a_{\mathrm{w}}$. In contrast, all three controls degrade scenario tracking
and substantially increase AC residuals. Thus, the CSP gain cannot be
explained by low dimensionality or spatial smoothing alone, but depends on
the orientation of the training-solution subspace.

\subsection{Post-training CSP correction}

Table~\ref{tab:intervention} compares raw predictions, calibration ($C$),
projection ($P_{16}$), and CSP$_{16}$ on frozen outputs from PIGNN-GC,
GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA. Relative to $C$, CSP reduces Mean
PB by $67.0\%$, $37.8\%$, $40.5\%$, and $68.9\%$, respectively, while
lowering voltage-magnitude RMSE in all four models. Projection also
increases $R_{\mathrm{w}}^2$ with little change in $a_{\mathrm{w}}$.
Angle accuracy is model dependent, degrading for the first three models
but improving for LUMINA. Measured raw and CSP$_{16}$ inference times show that CSP adds
negligible inference-time burden across all four models.

Comparing magnitude-only, angle-only, and full projection reveals that
the contribution of angle projection is model dependent. For PIGNN-GC,
full CSP gives the lowest PB among the projection variants, whereas
GridSFM achieves its lowest PB with magnitude-only projection.
For gridfm-graphkit, adding angle projection to the magnitude-only
correction reduces Mean/Max PB from $.0612/18.23$ to $.0457/5.08$,
but increases angle RMSE from $.367^\circ$ to $.496^\circ$.
For LUMINA, the same addition reduces Mean/Max PB from $.1939/38.53$
to $.0660/4.51$ while also improving angle RMSE from $1.374^\circ$
to $1.215^\circ$. Thus, improved physical consistency from angle projection does not
necessarily coincide with improved angle accuracy.

These model-dependent accuracy changes are explained by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tradeoff}. For magnitude, the removed off-subspace
prediction-error energy exceeds the discarded reference variation
across all four models. For angle, the balance differs across models:
in gridfm-graphkit, angle projection discards more valid reference
variation than model error and therefore worsens angle RMSE, whereas
the opposite holds for LUMINA.

\subsection{Complementarity with one-step Newton refinement.}

We test CSP before one Newton update ($\mathrm{NR}_1$) of non-slack angles
and PQ voltage magnitudes; validation selects $\eta=1$ for all models, with
no linear-solve failures. CSP initialization further lowers magnitude and
angle RMSE in all four models and reduces post-update Mean PB by
$21.1$--$96.4\%$ relative to $C$ initialization (Table~\ref{tab:nr1}).

For LUMINA, $C+\mathrm{NR}_1$ exhibits a large one-seed overshoot despite
a successful linear solve, whereas CSP$_{16}$ initialization is consistent
across all seeds, yielding Mean PB
$1.653\pm0.036\times10^{-3}$. This further supports CSP as a favorable
initializer for Newton refinement.

\subsection{Cross-grid breadth and distribution shift}
\label{sec:breadth_shift}
\noindent\textit{Cross-grid breadth.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:crossgrid_csp} evaluates GridSFM with CSP across 31 systems
spanning 4--9,241 buses and sub-kV to 750-kV levels: 28
pandapower/PYPOWER-format systems from IEEE, PEGASE, RTE, and GB benchmark
families, plus three Common Information Model (CIM)-based networks.
For each system, calibration and subspace statistics are fitted from its
training data, using nominal $k=16$ per channel and the available SVD
components on smaller systems. CSP reduces GridSFM Mean PB across all
31 systems.

\vspace{0.35em}

\noindent\textit{Topology and spatial shifts.}
Under $N$--1/$N$--2 outages, tracking and bulk PB remain close to control,
with larger maxima indicating isolated topology-induced violations and the
need for worst-case assessment beyond average error
\cite{chevalier2022guarantees}.
GBcorr, which applies correlated, spatially varying load-scaling factors
across 14 zones while preserving total demand and generation settings,
causes a broader shift: transferring both control calibration and
basis gives Mean PB $\approx0.100$ p.u. Refitting calibration alone reduces
this to $0.0944$, basis alone to $0.0740$, and both to $\approx0.068$.
The transferred basis retains $93.55\%$ of GBcorr magnitude variation,
versus $98.53\%$ after refitting, while reference-state projection reduces
Mean PB from $0.0868$ to $0.0438$ p.u. This shows that GBcorr introduces
not only calibration drift but also solution-subspace mismatch.

\section{CONCLUSION}

This work identifies output-error geometry as an important factor in the
accuracy--consistency gap of neural AC-PF surrogates. Fixed-norm and
Jacobian analyses reveal strong directional sensitivity of AC residuals,
while CSP reduces power-balance residuals and improves voltage-magnitude
accuracy on GBnetwork. CSP also improves voltage accuracy and mean power-balance residuals after one Newton update, supporting its complementary role in
residual-directed refinement. Cross-grid evaluation shows that the power-balance
improvement extends beyond GBnetwork, whereas distribution-shift experiments
show that a fixed solution subspace can become mismatched to the shifted
operating distribution. These results motivate extending physics-consistent
neural surrogates toward broader power-system analysis, estimation, and
optimization.
