\begin{abstract}
Recent neural power-flow solvers, including emerging foundation models,
achieve accurate voltage predictions, yet such accuracy does not
necessarily imply physically consistent solutions.
Even small complex voltage errors can yield large AC power-balance residuals.
We study this accuracy--consistency gap across PIGNN-GC, GridSFM,
gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA on realistic 2224-bus GBnetwork scenarios,
with cross-grid evaluation of GridSFM over 31 systems.
Using an SVD basis fitted to training AC power-flow solutions, we find
that neural prediction errors contain substantial components outside the
dominant solution subspace. To address this mismatch, calibrated
solution-subspace projection (CSP) suppresses off-subspace prediction
components after train-only bias calibration, reducing Mean PB by $67.0\%$, $37.8\%$, $40.5\%$, and $68.9\%$
for PIGNN-GC, GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA, respectively,
relative to calibrated predictions, while improving voltage-magnitude
accuracy in all four models.
These results identify output-error geometry as an important factor in
physics-consistent neural AC power flow.
Code: \href{http://github.com/Kimchangheon/neural-acpf-error-geometry}{github.com/Kimchangheon/neural-acpf-error-geometry}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

AC power flow (AC-PF) is a fundamental tool for steady-state power-system
analysis \cite{zimmerman2011matpower}, repeatedly evaluated in time-series
and contingency studies \cite{bolz2023contingency} and underlying optimal power flow (OPF)
\cite{frank2012opf, owerko2020opf} and state-estimation workflows
\cite{cheng2024stateestimation}.
This computational burden has motivated neural surrogates including direct data-driven approximation~\cite{owerko2024unsupervised,xu2025opformer,donon2020graphsolver, lin2024powerflownet}, physics- or constraint-aware training \cite{wen2026pigat,jiang2025upinn}, learned projection-based correction \cite{chzhen2026fab}, and feasibility-seeking mechanisms \cite{nguyen2025fsnet,donti2021dc3}.
More recent cross-grid and
foundation-model efforts---including GridSFM \cite{yang2026gridsfm},
gridfm-graphkit and GENCO \cite{gridfmgraphkit2026,puech2026genco},
and LUMINA \cite{li2026lumina}---extend this direction with global context
and topology-aware representations of heterogeneous grid components \cite{dejongh2022physics} designed to operate
across diverse grids and operating conditions \cite{zheng2022glassoformer, karim2026domainadaptation}. However, accurate voltage
prediction does not necessarily imply physical consistency
\cite{ma2026safepowergraph, shamseldein2026hybrid}. Physics-informed objectives can reduce
power-flow residuals \cite{nellikkath2022pinn, hu2021physicsguided}, but remain soft training
objectives and do not by themselves enforce exact satisfaction of the nonlinear AC equations \cite{kim2026pignn}.
Consequently, small voltage errors can coexist with non-negligible
power-balance violations. This raises a question beyond state accuracy:
which properties of the remaining voltage error govern
AC power-balance residuals? We hypothesize that error \emph{direction} is an important factor:
similarly sized errors can induce very different residuals depending on
their orientation across buses.

Goodwin et al.~\cite{goodwin2026geometry} characterized the differential
geometry and linear approximations of the AC-PF solution manifold. We study neural-surrogate error geometry empirically, asking
whether error orientation relative to a training-derived subspace
explains the remaining physical inconsistency. This also differs from
Park et al.~\cite{park2024compact}, who use a principal component analysis based output space during training and to warm-start an exact solver, whereas we use a training-solution subspace to analyze and post-process frozen AC-PF predictions.

We evaluate PIGNN-GC, GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA on realistic
2224-bus GBnetwork scenarios. Using training solutions only, we construct
separate singular value decomposition (SVD) subspaces for voltage magnitude and angle and decompose
calibrated prediction errors into in- and off-subspace components.
Fixed-norm interventions and local Jacobian analysis show that
off-subspace errors are systematically more residual-sensitive.
Motivated by this observation, we introduce \emph{calibrated
solution-subspace projection} (CSP), which combines train-only bias
correction with projection onto the dominant training-supported solution
directions.

Across all four models, CSP reduces AC power-balance residuals while
improving voltage-magnitude accuracy, with model-dependent effects on
angle accuracy. CSP adds negligible computational burden at inference and remains
beneficial when used to initialize one Newton refinement step.
Additional GridSFM evaluation across 31 systems shows that the benefit
extends beyond GBnetwork, whereas distribution-shift experiments reveal
that the training-solution subspace can become mismatched to a shifted
operating distribution.


\section{Error Geometry and CSP}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{AC power-balance consistency and calibration}

For an $N$-bus grid, let $x=[V,\theta]\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}$ denote the
voltage state, with $\underline V=V\odot e^{\jmath\theta}$.
Given operating condition $u$, a surrogate predicts $\hat{x}=f_\phi(u)$, from which $(P,Q)$
are recomputed using the network admittance model. For setpoints
$(P^{\mathrm{set}},Q^{\mathrm{set}})$, we define
\[
r(\hat x;u)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\Delta P\\ \Delta Q
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
P^{\mathrm{set}}-P\\
Q^{\mathrm{set}}-Q
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathrm{PB}=\sqrt{\Delta P^2+\Delta Q^2}.
\]
PB uses $\Delta P$ at PV/PQ and $\Delta Q$ at PQ buses, zero elsewhere;
prescribed PV/slack magnitudes and the slack angle are restored before
scoring. Mean/Max PB aggregate all bus--scenario pairs on a common
100-MVA per unit (p.u.) base.

Before geometric analysis, we remove train-only prediction bias:
\[
\hat b=\mathbb{E}_{s\in\mathrm{tr}}[\hat x_s-x_s^\star],
\qquad
\hat x^C=\hat x-\hat b,
\]
with $\hat b$ frozen at test time; angle offsets use circular differences
after gauge alignment.

\subsection{Error geometry and training solution subspace projection}

From training Newton--Raphson solutions, we construct rank-$k$
bases for centered magnitudes $X_V$ and gauge-aligned angles $X_\theta$:
\begin{equation}
X_V=W_V\Sigma_VU_V^\top,\qquad
X_\theta=W_\theta\Sigma_\theta U_\theta^\top,\qquad
P_k=W_kW_k^\top,
\end{equation}
where $W_k=\operatorname{diag}(W_{V,k},W_{\theta,k})$ contains the
leading $k$ directions per channel; $k=16$ denotes 16 per channel.
% These subspaces capture dominant training-solution variation and define the error coordinates.

For calibrated error $e=\hat x^C-x^\star$,
\begin{equation}
e^{\parallel}=P_ke,\quad
e^{\perp}=(I-P_k)e,\qquad
\hat x-x^\star=\hat b+e^{\parallel}+e^{\perp}.
\label{eq:error_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Here, $\hat b$ is the train-estimated mean bias, while
$e^{\parallel}$ and $e^{\perp}$ partition the remaining error into
in- and off-subspace components.

Around a converged state with fixed topology and bus types,
\begin{equation}
r(x^\star+e;u)
=
r(x^\star;u)+J(x^\star;u)e+\mathcal{O}(\|e\|_2^2),
\label{eq:local_residual}
\end{equation}
where $r(x^\star;u)=0$ and $J$ is the AC residual Jacobian; hence
equal-norm errors can yield different residuals by orientation.

% For calibrated error $e=\hat x^C-x^\star$,
% \begin{equation}
% e^{\parallel}=P_k e,\qquad
% e^{\perp}=(I-P_k)e,
% \label{eq:error_decomposition}
% \end{equation}
% are its in- and off-subspace components. Around a converged state with
% fixed topology and bus types,
% \begin{equation}
% r(x^\star+e;u)
% =
% J(x^\star;u)e+\mathcal{O}(\|e\|_2^2),
% \label{eq:local_residual}
% \end{equation}
% where $r(x^\star;u)=0$ and $J$ is the AC residual Jacobian; hence
% equal-norm errors can yield different residuals by orientation.

Motivated by this orientational sensitivity, CSP first removes $\hat{b}$ and then projects onto the dominant training-solution directions:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\hat x^{\mathrm{CSP}}
=
\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}}
+
P_k\left(\hat x-\hat b-\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}}\right)
}
\label{eq:csp}
\end{equation}
where $\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}}$ is the training-solution mean;
both $\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $P_k$ remain fixed at test time.
Projection can remove prediction error but also discard valid reference
variation. Let
$e^C=\hat x^C-x^\star$ and
$e^{\mathrm{CSP}}=\hat x^{\mathrm{CSP}}-x^\star$.
Since $P_k$ is orthogonal,
\begin{equation}
\|e^{\mathrm{CSP}}\|_2^2-\|e^C\|_2^2
=
\|(I-P_k)(x^\star-\bar x_{\mathrm{tr}})\|_2^2
-\|(I-P_k)e^C\|_2^2 .
\label{eq:tradeoff}
\end{equation}
Hence, CSP improves state accuracy when the removed off-subspace
prediction-error energy exceeds the discarded reference variation.

CSP is a lightweight, data-driven post-hoc output correction, not a projection onto the
nonlinear AC-feasible set, and therefore does not guarantee exact feasibility.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Our primary study uses 37,022 converged pandapower Newton--Raphson
operating points on the 2224-bus GBnetwork, split 1:1:1 for
training/validation/test. The corpus combines global load/generation
scaling with independent load $P/Q$ and active-generation perturbations;
the backbone topology and PV-voltage control schedule remain fixed.
We evaluate PIGNN-GC, our global-context extension of
PIGNN-Attn-LS~\cite{kim2026pignn} retaining its line-search, GridSFM~\cite{yang2026gridsfm},
gridfm-graphkit~\cite{gridfmgraphkit2026}, and
LUMINA~\cite{li2026lumina}; GridSFM is also evaluated
cross-grid on 31 systems.


All models are trained from scratch using model-specific stable
initialization, and CSP is evaluated within each frozen model.
% Unless otherwise stated, results are averaged over three model seeds; single-seed diagnostic analyses use one fixed seed.
gridfm-graphkit uses its released architecture/configuration without a
pretrained checkpoint. Both GridSFM and LUMINA retain their released backbones and are adapted
from AC-OPF to AC-PF by fixing generator dispatch, predicting only voltage
states with PF-specific residuals, and applying known-variable masking.
All models use pre-solution pandapower~\cite{zimmerman2011matpower,thurner2018pandapower} internal PPC-derived inputs and a common PPC-based AC power-balance residual evaluator. Training and inference use one NVIDIA H100 96-GB GPU; inference uses
the largest fitting batch size. Calibration statistics and solution
bases use training data only. From $k\in\{4,8,16,32,64\}$, validation
Mean PB selects $k^\star=16$ without degrading calibrated
voltage-magnitude RMSE and is frozen for test and distribution-shift experiments.
Unless otherwise stated, mechanistic, ablation, and cross-grid analyses
use model seed 42, whereas Tables~1 and~3 aggregate seeds 41--43.

Voltage RMSE alone can be small even when a model largely predicts a
static per-bus profile rather than following scenario-dependent voltage
variation~\cite{yang2026gridsfm}. To isolate this scenario-dependent
response, we center each bus as
$\tilde{V}_{is}=V_{is}-\bar{V}_i$ and
$\tilde{\hat{V}}_{is}=\hat{V}_{is}-\bar{\hat{V}}_i$, and report
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{w}}^2
=
\operatorname{corr}^2
\left(\tilde{V}_{is},\tilde{\hat{V}}_{is}\right),
\qquad
a_{\mathrm{w}}
=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}
\left(\tilde{V}_{is},\tilde{\hat{V}}_{is}\right)}
{\operatorname{Var}\left(\tilde{V}_{is}\right)} .
\end{equation}
$R_{\mathrm{w}}^2$ is the squared correlation between the centered
prediction and reference pooled over all bus--scenario pairs, while
$a_{\mathrm{w}}$ measures the scale of the predicted variation
relative to the true variation.

\subsection{Off-subspace error and directional sensitivity.}

\noindent\textit{Off-subspace error.}
Off-subspace error is substantial across models and is positively associated
with Mean PB. Although the rank-16 basis explains 98.8\%/99.7\% of training
voltage-magnitude/angle variation, the median fractions of total squared
error lying outside the respective subspaces are
26.3/55.0/56.8/27.1\% for magnitude and
10.3/2.7/39.6/31.7\% for angle, for
PIGNN-GC/GridSFM/gridfm-graphkit/LUMINA, respectively.
After accounting for differences in total error norms, scenarios with larger
off-subspace fractions have higher Mean PB, with partial Spearman correlations
of .659/.635/.594/.833 for magnitude and
.789/.906/.809/.860 for angle (all 95\% CIs exclude zero).

\noindent\textit{Fixed-norm intervention.}
At fixed error norm, suppressing off-subspace error consistently lowers
Relative Mean PB across all four surrogates, whereas suppressing in-subspace
error has the opposite effect (Fig.~\ref{fig:error_geometry}), isolating
error orientation from error magnitude. We test this with an oracle
diagnostic using the known reference state, varying the voltage-magnitude
error orientation at fixed $\ell_2$ norm:
\begin{equation}
\tilde e(\alpha)
=
\|e_V\|_2
\frac{e_V^\parallel+\alpha e_V^\perp}
{\|e_V^\parallel+\alpha e_V^\perp\|_2}.
\label{eq:controlled_geometry}
\end{equation}
Decreasing $\alpha$ suppresses off-subspace error; the control analogously
suppresses the in-subspace component by exchanging
$e_V^\parallel$ and $e_V^\perp$. Phase angles remain at their calibrated
predictions, and Mean PB is normalized at $\alpha=1$.

\noindent\textit{Jacobian sensitivity.}
Off-subspace directions are substantially more residual-sensitive than
in-subspace directions under the local AC Jacobian, consistent with the
fixed-norm intervention. Using directional gain
$g(d)=\|Jd\|_2/\|d\|_2$, 32 random direction pairs at each of 128 held-out
operating points yield a median paired off-/in-subspace gain ratio of
$1{,}563\times$. The same gain contrast appears in the actual calibrated
prediction errors: the ratios of median off- to in-subspace gains are
$22.81\times$, $575.83\times$, $4.69\times$, and $6.51\times$ for
PIGNN-GC, GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA, respectively.
The observed error regime is consistent with the first-order expansion in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_residual}, with median linearized-to-nonlinear
residual-change ratios between $0.996$ and $1.009$ across all four models.

\subsection{Solution subspace specificity.}
Our matched-control experiments show that preserving the same amount of
reference-state variation is insufficient for low PB, indicating that the
specific state-space directions retained by the solution subspace matter.
We compare SVD$_{16}$ with graph-Laplacian and spatial low-pass controls,
each tuned using training references to match the $V/\theta$ variation
retained by SVD$_{16}$. On the test set, all three preserve nearly identical
reference variation (Table~\ref{tab:basis_control}), yet the controls yield
substantially larger PB for both projected references and corrected predictions.

\subsection{Post-training CSP correction}

Table~\ref{tab:intervention} compares raw predictions, calibration ($C$),
projection ($P_{16}$), and CSP$_{16}$ on frozen outputs from PIGNN-GC,
GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA. Relative to $C$, CSP reduces Mean
PB by $67.0\%$, $37.8\%$, $40.5\%$, and $68.9\%$, respectively, while
lowering voltage-magnitude RMSE in all four models.
Projection improves $R_w^2$ with little change in $a_w$; angle
accuracy is model dependent. Raw and CSP16 inference times are
comparable.

Magnitude-only, angle-only, and full projection show model-dependent effects of
angle projection. PIGNN-GC achieves the lowest PB with full CSP, whereas
GridSFM performs best with magnitude-only projection. For gridfm-graphkit,
adding angle projection reduces Mean/Max PB from $.0612/18.23$ to $.0457/5.08$,
but increases angle RMSE from $.367^\circ$ to $.496^\circ$. For LUMINA, it
reduces Mean/Max PB from $.1939/38.53$ to $.0660/4.51$ while improving angle RMSE
from $1.374^\circ$ to $1.215^\circ$. Thus, better physical consistency from
angle projection need not imply better angle accuracy.


These model-dependent accuracy changes are explained by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tradeoff}. For magnitude, the removed off-subspace
prediction-error energy exceeds the discarded reference variation
across all four models. For angle, the balance differs across models:
in gridfm-graphkit, angle projection discards more valid reference
variation than model error and therefore worsens angle RMSE, whereas
the opposite holds for LUMINA.

\subsection{Complementarity with one-step Newton refinement.}

CSP provides a consistently better initialization for one-step Newton
refinement, improving both state accuracy and post-update physical
consistency across all four models. Starting from $C$ or CSP$_{16}$, we
apply one Newton update ($\mathrm{NR}_1$) to non-slack angles and PQ voltage
magnitudes, with validation-selected damping factor; $\eta=1$ is
selected for all models, with no linear-solve failures. Relative to $C$
initialization, CSP lowers magnitude and angle RMSE in all models and
reduces post-update Mean PB by $21.1$--$96.4\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:nr1}).

The benefit is particularly pronounced for LUMINA: $C+\mathrm{NR}_1$
shows a large one-seed overshoot despite a successful linear solve, whereas
CSP$_{16}$ initialization remains consistent across seeds.

\subsection{Cross-grid breadth and distribution shift}
\label{sec:breadth_shift}

\noindent\textit{Cross-grid breadth.}
CSP reduces GridSFM Mean PB across all 31 evaluated systems
(Fig.~\ref{fig:crossgrid_csp}), spanning 4--9,241 buses and sub-kV to
750-kV levels. The set includes 28 pandapower/PYPOWER-format systems
from IEEE, PEGASE, RTE, and GB benchmark families, plus three Common
Information Model (CIM)-based networks. For each system, calibration and
subspace statistics are fitted from its training data, using nominal
$k=16$ per channel and the available SVD components on smaller systems.
PV-voltage setpoints vary for most systems, but remain fixed for GBnetwork due to conflicting controllers.

\vspace{0.35em}

\noindent\textit{Topology and spatial shifts.}
CSP remains stable in bulk performance under $N$--1/$N$--2 topology
shifts, with degradation concentrated in isolated worst-case violations,
whereas GBcorr causes a broader performance drop.
Under the topology shifts, voltage accuracy, tracking, and bulk PB remain
close to control, while larger maxima motivate worst-case assessment beyond
average error~\cite{piloto2024canos}. GBcorr applies correlated, spatially
varying load scaling across 14 zones while preserving total demand and
generation settings. Transferring both calibration and basis gives Mean PB
$\approx0.100$ p.u.; refitting calibration alone reduces this to $0.0944$,
basis alone to $0.0740$, and both to $\approx0.068$.
Although the transferred/refitted bases retain $93.55\%/98.53\%$ of GBcorr
magnitude variation, projecting converged GBcorr references yields Mean PBs
of $0.0868/0.0438$ p.u., respectively. This reference-only diagnostic
isolates the effect of basis transfer from surrogate error and helps explain
the improvement from basis refitting.

\section{CONCLUSION}

This work identifies output-error geometry as an important factor in the accuracy--consistency gap of neural AC-PF surrogates. Fixed-norm and Jacobian analyses reveal strong directional sensitivity of AC residuals, while CSP reduces power-balance residuals and improves voltage-magnitude accuracy on GBnetwork. After one Newton update, CSP further improves voltage accuracy and mean power-balance residuals, supporting its complementary role in refinement. Cross-grid evaluation shows that the power-balance improvement extends beyond GBnetwork, whereas distribution-shift experiments show that a fixed solution subspace can become mismatched to the shifted operating distribution. These results motivate extending physics-consistent neural surrogates toward broader power-system analysis, estimation, and optimization.
