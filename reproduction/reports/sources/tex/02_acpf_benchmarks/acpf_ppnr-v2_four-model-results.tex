\section{Batch sizes and the rule that set them}

Batch size was never chosen per grid by hand. It follows one formula, applied
by \texttt{dispatch\_pf\_v2.sh}:
\[
B(\text{model},\text{grid})
  \;=\;\min\!\Big(\text{CAP},\;
  \Big\lfloor \frac{\text{BUDGET}\times M/48}{N}\Big\rfloor\Big),
\]
where $N$ is the bus count, $M$ the GPU memory in GiB, and BUDGET a per-model
allowance in \emph{bus-samples} calibrated so that $B\cdot N$ activations fit a
48~GiB card with headroom. The budgets differ because the measured activation
cost per bus-sample differs by a factor of seven across the four models.

\paragraph{The cap binds up to case1354pegase; past it, memory does.} Every
model runs at its cap on the first 21 grids, so nothing about batch size
distinguishes them there. From case1888rte on, $B$ falls with $1/N$: by
case9241pegase \texttt{pignn} is at 5 graphs per step, \texttt{gridsfm} at 6 and
\texttt{graphkit} at 12 --- reductions of 6.4$\times$, 5.3$\times$ and
5.3$\times$ against their small-grid batches.

\textbf{This confounds the size comparison.} Gradient noise scales as
$1/\sqrt{B}$, so the largest grids were optimised under roughly 2.5 times more
noise than the smallest, at the same learning rate and the same 40-epoch budget.
Any degradation reported above for $N \geq 2000$ mixes the difficulty of the
grid with a weaker optimiser setting, and the two are not separated by these
runs.

\texttt{lumina} is the exception: its activation cost is low enough that its
budget never binds, so it held $B=32$ on all 31 grids. It is the only model here
whose optimiser saw a constant batch across the corpus --- and also the only one
whose error is flat in $N$, which is consistent with it being limited by
something other than batch size.

\paragraph{This is now adjustable.} All four drivers take
\texttt{-{}-grad\_accum\_steps} and \texttt{-{}-target\_effective\_batch};
the latter derives the accumulation count from \texttt{-{}-BATCH} so the
effective batch can be held fixed across grids without more memory. Gradient
clipping moved to once per accumulation group. Neither flag was used for the
runs reported here, all of which ran at accumulation 1.

\section{Two failure modes, both identified}

\paragraph{Ten LUMINA runs died on a missing file.} Every LUMINA job on the alex
tier failed at \texttt{make\_model} with \texttt{FileNotFoundError} on
\texttt{lumina\_ckpt/lumina\_config.json}. LUMINA reads its architecture from
the released config even when training from scratch, and the file existed only
under the b313dc11 account while the dispatcher built the path from
\texttt{\$HOME\_BASE}. Copying it to the iwi5295h account and resubmitting fixed
all ten; they now train normally.

\paragraph{Twelve runs hit the 24-hour wall clock, and the fix for it failed.}
All twelve are among the five largest grids, where a single epoch costs
2900--6700 s, so 40 epochs needs 32--74 h:

helma's \texttt{preempt} partition allows 48 h on the same h100s, so all twelve
were resubmitted there. \textbf{Every one was evicted within three hours}
(elapsed 2:44 to 3:03, state \texttt{PREEMPTED}), and since the drivers have no
resume, that work was lost outright --- a worse outcome than the 24 h runs it
was meant to replace. Choosing preempt on its time limit alone, without
weighting how contended it is, was the wrong call.

They are now back on \texttt{h100} with the epoch budget cut to what fits ~21 h
at each run's measured per-epoch cost: pignn 14--25, graphkit 24, gridsfm
11--23, lumina 30, against 40 everywhere else. \textbf{Those twelve cells will
therefore carry a smaller training budget than the rest of the report}, and are
not equal-budget comparisons. They are still training and do not appear in the
tables below.

\paragraph{Residual reducer convention.}
The legacy log names \texttt{dPinf}/\texttt{dQinf} and the earlier notation
$\Delta P^\infty/\Delta Q^\infty$ can suggest a global test-set maximum.  The
driver actually computes a maximum over buses for each scenario, sums those
values, and divides by the number of scenarios.  Therefore all values in the
preceding table are \emph{mean per-scenario maxima}.  By contrast,
$\overline{|\Delta P|}$, RMSE and tolerance fractions pool all valid
bus--scenario entries.  Median/p95 are pooled quantiles (with at most two
million uniformly spaced entries retained by the older GridFM-derived driver
on very large arrays).  A true global maximum $\max_{s,i}|\Delta P_{s,i}|$ is
not reported in this document.

\section{$R^2_{\sigma}$: how far is the prediction from the solver?}

All $R^2$ in this section are $R^2_{\sigma}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:r2notation}: $1-(\mathrm{RMSE}/\sigma)^2$ against a per-bus
constant predictor.  It is not a tracking measure --- it also charges the model
for bias and amplitude error --- and the tracking coefficient
$R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ is reported separately there.


RMSE says how far a prediction sits from the reference. It does not say whether
the model responds to its input. Bus voltage magnitude occupies a narrow band,
so a model that ignores the scenario and emits the corpus-mean profile still
scores an RMSE equal to the spread of the data. On this corpus that spread,
$\sigma$, is between $1.5\times10^{-3}$ and $4.7\times10^{-2}$~pu depending on
the grid --- squarely inside the range several models report.

The coefficient of determination separates the two. Pooling over all buses and
all test scenarios,
\[
R^2_{\sigma} \;=\; 1 - \frac{\mathrm{SS}_{\text{res}}}{\mathrm{SS}_{\text{tot}}}
      \;=\; 1 - \Big(\frac{\text{RMSE}}{\sigma}\Big)^{2},
\]
which is exact, not an approximation: $\mathrm{SS}_{\text{res}} = N\,\text{RMSE}^2$
and $\mathrm{SS}_{\text{tot}} = N\,\sigma^2$ by definition.
$R^2_{\sigma} = 1$ is a perfect fit, $R^2_{\sigma} = 0$ is exactly as good as
the per-bus constant predictor, and $R^2_{\sigma} < 0$ is worse than it.

$\sigma$ was measured directly from the reference solutions of each grid (300
scenarios, all buses); angles are taken about the circular mean so a bus near
$\pm\pi$ does not acquire a spurious spread. No model is involved in $\sigma$.

that tracks and is miscalibrated}, and Section~\ref{sec:rescore} shows the
second is mostly what is happening: on $R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ most of these cells

themselves and therefore a pass over each saved checkpoint. Those passes have
since run for all four models on all 31 grids, and the results are in
Section~\ref{sec:rescore}. A slope near zero with $R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ near
zero is collapse; a slope near one with low $R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ is an unbiased
but noisy model; a slope well below one with a high $R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ ---
the common case here --- is a model that tracks and under-states the spread.
$R^2_{\sigma}$ settles none of the three.

\section{Notation: three different $R^2$, and the calibration}
\label{sec:r2notation}

Three distinct coefficients appear in the literature and in this project's
logs, and earlier revisions of this report used the word ``$R^2$'' for more
than one of them.  Every table below names which one it is.  Write $v$ for the
reference $|V|$ at a bus in a scenario, $\hat v$ for the prediction,
$\bar v_i$ for bus $i$'s mean over scenarios, and fit
$\hat v = a\,v + b + \varepsilon$ by least squares.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Symbol & Definition & Null model it scores $0$ against & Calibration\\
\midrule
$R^2_{\mathrm{pool}}$ & Pearson $R^2$ of $\hat v$ on $v$, pooled over
  & one number for every bus & changes\\
  & all buses and scenarios & and scenario & \\[2pt]
$R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ & the same after subtracting $\bar v_i$
  & each bus at its own mean & \textbf{invariant}\\
  & from $\hat v$ and $v$ alike & & \\[2pt]
$R^2_{\sigma}$ & $1-\sum(\hat v-v)^2/\sum(v-\bar v_i)^2$
  & each bus at its own mean & changes\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

$R^2_{\mathrm{pool}}$ is the diagnostic GridSFM's section 6.2 defines, and it
is the right one for a multi-grid foundation model, which must reproduce a
static voltage profile it has never seen.  Every campaign here trains one model
per grid on that grid, where that profile is memorisable from the training
split; a predictor that emits each bus's training mean and ignores the scenario
entirely scores $R^2_{\mathrm{pool}} = 0.798$ on GBnetwork.  So
$R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ is the tracking question in this setting, and
$R^2_{\mathrm{pool}}$ is kept only for comparability with the published
numbers.

$R^2_{\sigma}$ is what earlier revisions of this report printed.  It is not a
tracking measure: it charges the model for bias and for amplitude error as
well.  Writing $\sigma^2=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum(v-\bar v_i)^2$ and
$\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ for the variance of $\varepsilon$, the mean square error
splits exactly into three terms,
\[
  \mathrm{RMSE}^2 \;=\; \underbrace{b^2}_{\text{bias}}
  \;+\;\underbrace{(a-1)^2\sigma^2}_{\text{amplitude}}
  \;+\;\underbrace{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}_{\text{noise}},
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad
  R^2_{\sigma} \;=\; 1-\frac{b^2}{\sigma^2}-(a-1)^2-\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{\sigma^2}.
\]
$R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ normalises the noise term by the variance of the
\emph{prediction} rather than of the reference, so it is not a simple
difference away from $R^2_{\sigma}$; the two answer different questions and
should not be converted into one another.

Three relations are used below, and each was checked against the measurements
rather than assumed.  (i) Calibration removes exactly the $b^2/\sigma^2$ term,
so $R^2_{\sigma,\mathrm{cal}} = 1-(a-1)^2-\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\sigma^2$; over
the 26 stabilization runs this held to $1.4\times10^{-2}$.  (ii)
$R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ and the slope are unchanged by calibration, since a
per-bus offset cannot alter a regression on per-bus deviations; measured
deviation $<10^{-6}$ on all 26.  (iii) $R^2_{\sigma,\mathrm{cal}} \le
R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$, with the gap being the amplitude error; observed on all
26, from $0.0006$ to $1.22$.

\paragraph{Post-hoc per-bus calibration.}  The $b^2/\sigma^2$ term is removable
without touching the model.  For each bus, take the mean prediction over the
\emph{training} split, align it to the training reference mean, and subtract
that fixed offset from every unseen test scenario:
\[
  \hat v^{\,\mathrm{cal}}_{i,s} \;=\; \hat v_{i,s}
  \;-\;\bigl(\overline{\hat v}^{\,\mathrm{train}}_{i}-\bar v^{\,\mathrm{train}}_{i}\bigr),
\]
and the circular equivalent for $\theta$.  No test data enters the fit, so this
is a correction a deployed model could carry.  Because it is fitted on one
split and applied to another it does not transfer perfectly: across the 26
stabilization runs the realised improvement in $R^2_{\sigma}$ differs from the
fitted $b^2/\sigma^2$ by a median of $1.2\%$ and at most $8.4\%$.  Tables that
report it use the form \emph{raw $\to$ calibrated}.  $R^2_{\mathrm{bus}}$ and the slope are
mathematically unchanged by it --- a per-bus offset cannot alter a regression
on per-bus deviations --- which is used below as a consistency check on the
implementation.

\section{Re-scored: what the $R^2$ tables above are and are not}
\label{sec:rescore}

The $R^2$ tables above are $1-(\mathrm{RMSE}/\sigma)^2$ with $\sigma$ measured
per bus, which is the right null for a per-grid surrogate but is \emph{not} the
diagnostic GridSFM's section 6.2 defines.  That one is a pooled regression of
prediction against reference reported with a slope, and its $R^2$ is a Pearson
$R^2$: it asks whether the model orders scenarios correctly, and is blind to
bias and to amplitude error.  The discussion below Table~\ref{tab:r2theta}
previously read the SSE quantity as if it answered the regression question.  It
does not, and where the two disagree they disagree by a lot.

All 31 grids have been re-scored from the saved checkpoints with both, plus
per-edge angle diagnostics (alex jobs 4125850/4126397; 112 model--grid cells,
3000 test scenarios each).  The rescore reproduces the earlier figures to every
printed digit where they overlap, and its independently measured $\sigma$
agrees with the hand-measured $\sigma$ of Table~\ref{tab:r2vmag} to a median of
$1.3\%$, which confirms the per-bus convention used throughout this report.

$64.6\%$ of $P$-scored buses have $P^{\mathrm{set}}$ exactly zero; the median
setpoint is $0.0000$~pu on both channels. A median residual therefore lands on
pure transit nodes, where a predictor that emits a valid AC solution of the
mean scenario is exact for free --- no injection, no mismatch, in any scenario
--- while a learned model perturbs the voltage everywhere and gets no such
gift. Median comparisons \emph{between models} are unaffected, since the
distortion is common to them; median comparisons \emph{against the constant
predictor} are not a measure of skill. The mean and the maximum are dominated
by the buses that carry injection and are the honest comparison there.

\paragraph{A calibration defect, not collapse.}  With
$\hat v = a v + b + \varepsilon$,
$\mathrm{RMSE}^2 = b^2 + (a-1)^2\sigma^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2$, so the gap
between the regression $R^2$ and the SSE $R^2$ is exactly
$(a-1)^2 + b^2/\sigma^2$: amplitude error plus bias, and nothing about
tracking.  On GBnetwork, where this was pursued in detail
(\texttt{acpf\_gbnetwork\_case300\_global-context-report}), the bias term alone
exceeds $\sigma$ for every model, and subtracting a per-bus offset fitted on
the training split --- no retraining, no architecture change --- cuts $|V|$
RMSE by $2.0$--$2.7\times$, turns every $R^2$ positive and improves median
$|\Delta Q|$ by $3$--$4\times$.  That correction has now been applied
corpus-wide as well (Section~\ref{sec:calcorpus}); the tables earlier in this
report stand as measured, but what they measure is a miscalibration a post-hoc
per-bus offset largely removes, not the absence of a learned mapping.

\section{PIGNN diverges on four grids}

On LVN\_heo1, case1888rte, GBnetwork and case2848rte the PIGNN run produced a
constant validation RMSE, and the cause is \emph{not} the v1 defect. The
zero-grad guard that killed 11 runs in v1 fired \textbf{zero times} here ---
\texttt{-{}-armijo\_mode geometric\_safe} kept the graph edge alive as intended.
What happens instead is divergence:

\begin{verbatim}
Epoch  0 | train loss 1.1116e+04  rmse 1.0297e+00  (dPinf 1.126e+05 pu)
Epoch 40 | train loss nan         rmse nan         (dPinf nan)
\end{verbatim}

The initial loss is already $1.1\times10^{4}$ with a mean scenario-maximum mismatch of
$1.1\times10^{5}$ pu, and the run reaches NaN. The reported validation RMSE is
constant because the best checkpoint never moves past epoch 0. These cells are
marked \textsc{nan} and excluded from the aggregates.

\textbf{This is a different failure from v1 and needs a different fix.} The v1
problem was no gradient; here the gradient exists and is too large.
\texttt{geometric\_safe} always applies a step, including one that increases the
residual, which plausibly contributes --- it is the trade-off noted when the
mode was chosen. Gradient clipping or a lower learning rate on these grids is
the obvious next experiment; neither has been run.

\section{Caveats}

\paragraph{Coverage is incomplete.} 22 of 31 grids have all four models:
graphkit and lumina stand at 30 usable, gridsfm at 26, pignn at 22. The gap is
the five largest grids, whose runs are described above.

\paragraph{Twelve cells will carry a smaller budget.} The resubmitted runs on
the five largest grids use 11--30 epochs against 40 elsewhere, because 40 does
not fit a 24 h limit at their measured cost and the 48 h alternative is
preempted.

\paragraph{Single seed.} Every number is one run. Differences below roughly a
factor of two should not be read as real; PIGNN's margins are far above that
threshold, the graphkit--gridsfm gap on $|V|$ is not.
